🌍 The Global Reality

Malaria is most prevalent in African and tropical Asian countries. It is often called a "disease of the developing world." While it can be cured if diagnosed and treated promptly, early symptoms mimic the flu, making diagnosis difficult.

🇺🇸 What About the U.S.?

🦟 Part II of IV

Mosquitoes: Flying Factories of Malaria

Understanding the biology behind the disease

🧫 Plasmodium — The Parasite Behind Malaria

Malaria is caused by single-celled protists in the genus Plasmodium. Only four species infect humans. Its life cycle requires two hosts: humans and the female Anopheles mosquito.

👤 In Humans

Plasmodium invades the liver, lymph nodes, and red blood cells. It replicates inside cells, causing them to rupture — producing characteristic fevers, nausea, and flu-like symptoms.

🦟 In Mosquitoes

Plasmodium lives in the gut and salivary glands of the female Anopheles mosquito with no harm to the mosquito. Only female mosquitoes bite — they need blood proteins for egg development.

🔄 How Transmission Works
  • 1️⃣An infected female Anopheles mosquito bites a person — injecting Plasmodium along with her saliva
  • 2️⃣Plasmodium travels to the liver and red blood cells, replicates, and causes cells to rupture — triggering fever symptoms
  • 3️⃣When another mosquito bites this infected person, it picks up Plasmodium — and the cycle continues
  • 🔁The parasite moves to the mosquito's salivary gland, ready to infect the next human

🛏️ Current Prevention Methods

Indoor insecticide spraying kills mosquitoes for 3–6 months. Insecticidal bed nets provide protection lasting 3–5 years. But as Baruti's story shows — nets are hot, and people don't always use them properly.

🦟 Part III of IV

A Novel Approach: Bacterial Transformation!

This is where lab science meets real-world problem solving

🌟 The Big Idea

Researchers have focused on engineering symbiotic bacteria inside the mosquito gut — using the same transformation techniques you practiced with pGLO!

🔬 The Wang et al. (2012) Experiment

Researchers engineered Pantoea agglomerans (which grows inside Anopheles mosquitoes) to express the hemolysin A (hlyA) system — proteins that cause red blood cells to lyse (burst).


  • 🍬Transformed bacteria were fed to mosquitoes through sugar solutions
  • 🩸When the mosquito bites an infected human, the toxins cause red blood cells to burst inside the mosquito gut
  • 💀This halts Plasmodium's life cycle — nearly 98% inhibition!
  • 🌟GFP was used to track bacteria multiplying in the gut — just like in your lab!

Prognosis: Back at Baruti's Bedside

Drug resistance, combination therapy, and the future of treatment

Baruti opens his eyes and sees his mother sitting at his side. She takes his hand and tells him she loves him. The doctors are cautiously optimistic — the mixture of antimalarial drugs is working. They expect him to recover.— From the Case Study

💊 How Are Antimalarial Drugs Used?

Most antimalarial drugs target the red blood cell (erythrocytic) stage. But Plasmodium can also lie dormant in the liver, meaning drugs targeting only the blood stage may not fully clear the parasite.

🔗 Cross-Resistance

Resistance to one drug can confer resistance to related drugs in the same chemical family — because the same biochemical mechanism handles multiple drugs.

💊 Combination Therapy

The standard is using two or more drugs with different modes of action together (e.g., ACT — Artemisinin-based Combination Therapy). This delays the development of resistance.
